What Makes Marvin Different

Marvin sits just south of Weddington along the Waxhaw corridor and shares much of the same large-lot, low-density development pattern. Incorporated in the mid-2000s partly to manage growth and preserve larger minimum lot sizes, Marvin has almost no public sewer infrastructure — private septic is the standard for essentially every residential property in town.

  • Large minimum lot sizes are standard. Like neighboring Weddington, Marvin's zoning favors larger residential lots, which generally gives septic systems more room for the drain field and easier repair access than tighter suburban lots.
  • Private well and septic together on nearly every property. With no public water or sewer utility reaching most of Marvin, homes rely on both a private well and a private septic system, so separation distance between the two is always a factor in any work we do.
  • Newer development alongside established larger estates. Marvin has seen steady newer construction in recent years alongside longer-established larger-acreage properties, meaning system age varies significantly by neighborhood.
  • Proximity to Waxhaw's growth corridor. Marvin borders Waxhaw's southern and western edges, an area seeing active new construction — new septic installations here follow current NC code and Union County permitting requirements.

New construction note: If you're building new in Marvin, a soil evaluation and Union County permit are required before installation. We coordinate this process from initial site evaluation through final inspection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do all Marvin homes have septic systems?
The large majority do. Marvin has essentially no public sewer utility, so private septic paired with a private well is the standard setup for residential properties.
How much does septic pumping cost in Marvin, NC?
Most Marvin pump-outs run a flat rate depending on tank size and access, with no deposit required to schedule.
Do you install new septic systems for new construction in Marvin?
Yes. We handle the full process — soil evaluation, system design, and Union County Environmental Health permitting — for new construction throughout Marvin.
How does Marvin's lot size affect septic system placement?
Marvin's larger lots generally provide more flexibility for drain field placement and required setbacks from wells and property lines, which can simplify design compared to tighter suburban lots elsewhere in the region.
How often should I pump my septic tank in Marvin?
Most households need pumping every 3–5 years, depending on household size and water use. We measure sludge levels at every visit to confirm the right interval for your system.
