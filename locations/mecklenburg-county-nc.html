Private Septic Near Charlotte: What to Know

If your Mecklenburg County property is on private septic, most systems need pumping every 3–5 years regardless of whether you're noticing problems. Edge-area lots and older homes near the Union County border often have tanks or drain fields that haven't been serviced in years. When buying or selling a home with a septic system, a written inspection gives both parties a clear picture of the system's condition before closing day.

Mecklenburg County is the most urbanized county Redline serves, but municipal sewer coverage isn't universal. Areas annexed by Charlotte after homes were already built and connected to septic often kept their systems — retrofitting sewer lines to an established neighborhood is expensive, and the county doesn't require a switch as long as the existing system passes inspection. The same is true for large-lot, rural-zoned pockets near the Union County line and older subdivisions built before sewer expansion reached them. If you're not sure whether your property is on public sewer or private septic, check your water bill for a sewer charge — no charge usually means septic.

Not sure if you're on septic? Call Redline at (704) 562-9922 and we can help you confirm before scheduling service.

Septic Permits in Mecklenburg County

New septic installations and most full-system replacements in Mecklenburg County require a permit and site/soil evaluation, administered through the county's environmental health division under North Carolina's statewide on-site wastewater rules. Frequently Asked Questions

Do all Mecklenburg County properties have septic systems?
No. Most of central Charlotte is on municipal water and sewer. Private septic systems are mainly found on larger lots, older homes, and properties on the edges of the county, especially near Matthews, Mint Hill, and the Union County border.
Is a permit required to repair or replace a septic system in Mecklenburg County?
Yes, for new installations and most full replacements. Septic systems in North Carolina are regulated under state on-site wastewater rules, administered locally through the county's environmental health division, which requires a permit and site evaluation before work begins.
Do you serve septic properties near Charlotte's city limits?
Yes. We serve private septic properties throughout Mecklenburg County, including edge-area homes near Charlotte, Matthews, Mint Hill, and Huntersville, alongside our core coverage in Union and Cabarrus Counties.
How much does a septic permit cost in Mecklenburg County, and how long does it take?
Permit fees are set by Mecklenburg County Public Health and vary by project type — new installation typically costs more than a repair or replacement permit. Site evaluation and permit issuance usually add 1–3 weeks before work can begin, depending on season and department workload. Redline handles the permit application and site evaluation coordination as part of every installation or replacement project.
Do you provide near-me septic service across all of Mecklenburg County?
Yes. We serve private septic properties throughout Mecklenburg County — not just Charlotte's edges, but Matthews, Mint Hill, Huntersville, and unincorporated pockets near the Union County line — with the same flat-rate pricing and same-day scheduling as our core Union County service area.
