Septic Repair Issues Common in Outlying Matthews

Sewage backups
Wastewater backing into drains, tubs, or toilets can come from a full tank, a blocked line, a collapsed pipe, or a drain field no longer accepting water.
Baffle deterioration
Many outlying Matthews homes have systems with concrete baffles now past their reliable service life. A failed outlet baffle sends solids into the drain field.
Broken inlet or outlet lines
Root intrusion, soil movement, and age cause cracking and collapse in septic lines, particularly in older systems.
Drain field problems
Wet or odorous areas in the yard, lush green patches over drain field lines, or slow drains across multiple fixtures indicate drain field stress or failure.
Tank access and riser issues
Cracked lids, sunken access covers, or buried risers without surface access are both safety hazards and barriers to proper maintenance.
Pre-sale repair work
Selling an outlying Matthews home with a private septic system? We help resolve inspection findings ahead of closing.

Why does my Matthews home have a septic system?
Much of central Matthews is on public sewer, but outlying homes on larger lots away from the town center, particularly toward the Union County and Stallings borders, developed before sewer lines reached them and still rely on private septic.
My Matthews home has a sewage odor — what does that mean?
Sewage odors inside the home can point to a dry trap, a failed wax ring, or a full septic tank. Odors outside, particularly near the drain field, often indicate the system is surfacing effluent, meaning the drain field may be failing or the tank is overloaded.
