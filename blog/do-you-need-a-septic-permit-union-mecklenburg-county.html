Do You Need a Permit for Septic Repair or Replacement in Union & Mecklenburg County, NC?

Repair & Installation August 28, 2026 By Redline Site Services — NC Licensed Septic Contractors 6 min read

If you're planning septic work in Union County or Mecklenburg County and searching "do I need a permit for this," the short answer depends entirely on what you're doing. Routine maintenance doesn't need one. Installation, replacement, and most structural repairs do. Here's exactly where that line falls, how the two counties' processes differ, and what happens if the work goes unpermitted.

The Short Answer: It Depends on the Work

North Carolina regulates septic systems under state on-site wastewater rules (NCGS Chapter 130A, §§130A-334 through 130A-340), administered locally through each county's environmental health division. The statute doesn't require a permit for every service call — it requires one when the work changes or restores how the system functions. That distinction is what separates "call and schedule" work from "apply for a permit first" work.

What Always Requires a Permit

  • New septic system installation — any first-time system on a property requires a permit and a site/soil evaluation before installation can begin.
  • Full or partial system replacement — replacing the tank, the drain field, or both requires a new permit, even on a property that already has a permitted system.
  • Drain field repair or expansion — repairing a failing drain field or adding capacity requires a permit in most cases, since it changes how effluent is dispersed.
  • Converting to an alternative system — switching to an aerobic treatment unit, drip irrigation, or another alternative system type requires a new permit even if the old system was permitted.

What Doesn't Require a Permit

  • Routine pumping or tank cleaning — a maintenance service, not construction. No permit needed.
  • Minor repairs — fixing a broken lid or riser, clearing a blocked line, or replacing an effluent filter typically doesn't require a permit, since it restores the existing system rather than altering it.
  • Inspections — a pre-sale, lender-required, or routine condition inspection is not construction work and doesn't require a permit.

The gray area: Larger repairs — replacing a distribution box, rerouting a section of drain line, or repairing (not replacing) part of a drain field — can go either way depending on scope and the inspector's assessment. Always confirm with your county's environmental health office, or ask your contractor to confirm, before work begins.

Union County vs. Mecklenburg County: How the Process Differs

Both counties enforce the same state rules, but the permit is issued and processed by separate offices, and each has its own site evaluation queue and fee schedule:

  • Union County permits are issued by Union County Environmental Health. This is Redline's core service area — Monroe, Indian Trail, Waxhaw, Wesley Chapel, Stallings, and surrounding communities.
  • Mecklenburg County permits are issued by Mecklenburg County Public Health's Environmental Health Division. This covers edge-area private septic properties near Matthews, Mint Hill, Huntersville, and the Union County border. See our Mecklenburg County service page for the full coverage area.

In both counties, the sequence is the same: a licensed contractor or the property owner submits a permit application, the county schedules a site/soil evaluation, and the permit is issued once the evaluation confirms the proposed system meets state requirements. Site evaluation and permit issuance typically add 1–3 weeks before physical work can start, though timelines vary by season and department workload — spring and early summer tend to run longer due to higher demand.

What Happens If Septic Work Was Done Without a Permit

Unpermitted septic work doesn't disappear — it resurfaces at the worst possible time, usually during a home sale, refinance, or a future service call when a contractor notices the system doesn't match county records. A few consequences worth knowing:

  1. It can delay or derail a closing. Buyers' agents and lenders increasingly check permit history. An unpermitted replacement or repair is a red flag that can stall a sale until it's resolved.
  2. You may have to retroactively permit or redo the work. If the county can't verify the system meets code, it may require a new site evaluation, a retroactive permit, or in some cases removing and redoing the work to code — all more expensive than doing it right the first time.
  3. It's a disclosure issue. As covered in our guide to what NC law requires for septic inspections, sellers must disclose known material defects — and an unpermitted system with no documented site evaluation is exactly the kind of issue a pre-listing inspection surfaces before it becomes a buyer's problem.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to repair my septic tank in Union County or Mecklenburg County, NC?

It depends on the scope. Minor repairs like fixing a broken lid or clearing a blocked line typically don't require a permit. Replacing the tank, distribution box, or drain field, or any structural repair, requires a permit from the county's environmental health department. When in doubt, call before starting work — Redline confirms permit requirements before quoting any repair.

Do I need a permit to pump or clean my septic tank?

No. Routine pumping and tank cleaning are maintenance services and do not require a permit in either county.

Who issues septic permits in Union County vs. Mecklenburg County?

Union County Environmental Health issues permits for Union County properties. Mecklenburg County Public Health's Environmental Health Division issues them for Mecklenburg County properties, including Matthews, Mint Hill, and Charlotte-area edge properties. Each office runs its own site evaluation and permitting process.

What happens if septic work was done without a required permit?

Unpermitted work can surface during a home sale, refinance, or future service call. It's a red flag for buyers and lenders, can require costly retroactive permitting or redoing the work to code, and may complicate closing. Always confirm permit status before buying a home with a private septic system.

How long does it take to get a septic permit in Union or Mecklenburg County?

Site evaluation and permit issuance typically take 1–3 weeks depending on the county, season, and project scope, though it can run longer during high-demand periods. Redline builds permit lead time into every installation and replacement project timeline.
