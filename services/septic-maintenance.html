Frequently Asked Questions

How often should a septic tank be maintained?
Most homes need septic pumping every 3-5 years, but tank size, household size, water use, and system age can shorten or extend that schedule.
What does septic maintenance include?
Septic maintenance usually includes pumping or cleaning when needed, checking sludge levels, inspecting baffles and lids, reviewing drain field warning signs, and setting the next service interval.
Can maintenance prevent septic repairs?
Yes. Routine maintenance helps catch full tanks, damaged baffles, access problems, and early drain field stress before they become expensive failures.
Is septic maintenance the same as septic pumping?
Pumping is one part of maintenance, not the whole thing. A maintenance visit includes the pump-out when it's due, plus a baffle check, access-lid check, and drain field review — and sets the interval for your next visit, so it's a broader check-up rather than a single task.
Do systems with a pump or alarm need different maintenance?
Yes. Pump-assisted and aerobic systems have mechanical components — pumps, floats, alarms — that need periodic function checks in addition to the standard tank maintenance a gravity system needs. If your system has an alarm panel, let us know when scheduling so we bring the right equipment.
